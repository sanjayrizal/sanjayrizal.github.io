Sizes DC or AC cable runs to keep voltage drop within an acceptable limit, and shows the resulting drop across every standard conductor size.

A  Circuit Parameters

Method. VD (V) = k × L × I × ρ ÷ A, where k = 2 for DC / single-phase (round trip) or √3 for three-phase, ρ is resistivity (Cu ≈ 0.0175, Al ≈ 0.0282 Ω·mm²/m), L is one-way length, and A is cross-section. Three-phase figures are resistive-only — for long runs, also check cable reactance against manufacturer data.

Minimum front-to-front row pitch for a fixed-tilt ground-mount array so rows don't shade each other at solar noon on the design day (worst case: winter solstice).

A  Site & Array Geometry

Method. Solar noon altitude α = 90° − |latitude − declination|. Shadow length = (panel length × sin tilt) ÷ tan α. Minimum pitch = panel ground footprint (length × cos tilt) + shadow length. Default declination (−23.45°) is the winter solstice worst case — override it to check a different design day.

Simple payback, lifetime savings, and CO₂ offset for a sized system — same categories reported in your PVsyst / SAM outputs, made editable.

A  System & Cost Parameters

Method. Simple payback = CAPEX ÷ (year-1 revenue − annual O&M). Lifetime figures apply annual module degradation to each year's generation before summing. Default grid emission factor (0.003 kgCO₂/kWh) matches the IEA lifecycle figure used in your own Bhyandol SAM report for Nepal's hydro-heavy grid — replace it for other countries.
